Think of it like looking through a window that slides across an array. Instead of checking every possible subarray (which would be O(n²) or worse), you maintain a "window" of elements and slide it efficiently across the data structure.
It’s a way to reduce time complexity by avoiding recalculating things you already know.
Instead of recalculating data in every loop iteration (which wastes time),
you "slide" a fixed-size or variable-size range (window) across the array,
updating results as you move — just adding new elements and removing old ones.

Types of Sliding Window
1) Fixed Window – Window size is constant (e.g., k = 3)
2) Dynamic Window – Window size changes depending on conditions
